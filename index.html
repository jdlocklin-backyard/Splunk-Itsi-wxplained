Distribution Center Service Health Model — Splunk ITSI

How KPIs from AppDynamics, Zenoss, LogistiVIEW, and Dematic roll up into a single operational health score

Splunk IT Service Intelligence (ITSI) uses service decomposition to break complex business operations into measurable services. Each service contains Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) — recurring searches that monitor specific metrics. ITSI calculates a weighted health score for each service based on KPI severity and importance, then propagates health through service dependencies to give operations leaders a single view of distribution center health.

🔎 How It's Calculated

Service Health Score = Σ (KPI_severity_value × KPI_importance) / Σ (KPI_importance)
Normal = 100 Low = 70 Medium = 50 High = 30 Critical = 0
Minimum Health Indicator (Importance = 11): If any KPI with importance 11 reaches Critical severity, the entire service health score drops to Critical (0), regardless of all other KPIs. This is ITSI's override mechanism for must-not-fail metrics.
Dependency Propagation: When services roll up to a parent, each child service's health score is treated as an importance-11 dependency. If any child service goes Critical, the parent score also drops to Critical.
Weighted Average: For non-critical states, the health score is a weighted average — KPIs with higher importance values have proportionally more influence on the service score.
KPIs are recurring Splunk searches that return performance metrics on a schedule. Each KPI maps to a specific aspect of service performance — response time, availability, throughput, error rate, etc.
Health scores range 0–100 where 100 = Normal (fully healthy) and 0 = Critical (complete failure). The score is color-coded to match ITSI's severity scale for instant visual recognition.
Importance 11 = minimum health indicator. If a KPI at this level goes Critical, it forces the entire parent service to Critical. Use this for metrics where failure means the service is effectively down.
Service dependencies propagate health: if a child service degrades, the parent reflects it. This creates a cascading model where a single KPI failure can ripple up to the executive dashboard.
